# Course grading and late policy

**Grade weights (ICS553, Fall 2026)**

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| PBL portfolios (Prosits 1–3, group deliverables + individual reflections) | 20% |
| Quizzes | 20% |
| Literature review (individual) | 10% |
| Research paper review (group) | 10% |
| **Final capstone project** | **40%** |

**What the first lecture said (spoken, older):** no written exams; the course is problem-based. The lecturer then gave the custom project as **50%**, done after the mid-semester break in small groups of about three with student-chosen topics. The written syllabus and brief say **40%**; trust the written figure. He also said the "derive algorithms from maths" outcome is assessed through quizzes, the technical documentation in portfolios, and model evaluation, so derivations must be written into the report.

A grade of **C or better** is needed to pass the course.

**Late work:** everything is due when stated; **−25% of the grade per day late**; after two days past the deadline it is not accepted (0%). This applies to the capstone repo (Fri 11 Dec) and report.

**Attendance:** lectures and labs are mandatory; illness needs a health professional's note; missing the equivalent of three weeks of classes can mean failure. Arriving more than 5 minutes late is recorded as late, and persistent lateness costs 1% of the final grade per additional instance.

**Other dates that compete with the capstone:** Research paper review assigned Week 12 and due Week 13.

**Quizzes (lecture 9).** Quizzes follow major topics; after unsupervised learning the next blocks were neural networks, then a light tour of advanced topics. Roughly two more quizzes were expected.

## For the capstone
Plan to push the repo a day early: one day late on a 40% component costs 10% of the course grade.
