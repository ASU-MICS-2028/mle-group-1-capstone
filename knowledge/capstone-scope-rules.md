# Capstone scope rules

Four tests a topic must pass:
1. **African-centred** — problem, data and decision context are African (Titanic/MNIST-style problems are rejected).
2. **Decision-relevant** — a real decision-maker who would act differently because of the model.
3. **About five weeks of part-time work** for four people; an unfinished mega-project scores worse than a finished modest one.
4. **Data-honest** — the team must know beforehand that it can legally and practically obtain the data.

**In scope:** tabular classification/regression with strong validation; clustering/dimensionality reduction with a decision use; a small NN if the data justifies it; time series with proper temporal validation; image/text models where labels and compute exist; a prototype (script, API, simple app, dashboard).

**Out of scope:** data you cannot obtain or share; uploading private/personal data to generative-AI tools; production-grade cloud services; scraping against site terms or law; purely descriptive work with no ML task; buying proprietary data.

**Scope changes** after approval need the instructor's agreement. Narrowing is usually fine; changing topic needs a short email explaining why. If data falls through, tell the instructor immediately rather than waiting for the mid-check. Deep learning is not required: a well-validated classical model is preferred over an unexplained network.

## For the capstone
Option B (flood early warning) was approved on 9 Nov 2026 and is now the contract. Switching to the galamsey fallback, or shrinking to Greater Accra, should be agreed with the instructor first.

**From the lecture-9 briefing.** The lecturer stressed: do not try to "solve Africa's problems" in five weeks; something small that works end to end beats a fancy unfinished system. He changed the rules live to allow a **small neural network where the data justifies it**, mainly as one more model to compare against the classical ones. **Synthetic data** is acceptable only if the proposal justifies it (e.g. real data is confidential) and you show it reflects the real application. Classical models need little compute; a GPU partner is available on request. Example areas he gave included flood or drought early warning, El Niño, crop prices, credit scoring, mobile-money fraud, dropout warning and speed cameras.
