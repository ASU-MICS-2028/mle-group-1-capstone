# Reproducible literature search strategy

Research should be reproducible. For a review, that means another person can follow your steps and end up with the same set of papers.

Steps the lecturer described:

1. **Build a search string** from your topic's keywords, combined with AND / OR (Boolean expressions).
2. **Search several databases**: e.g. ScienceDirect, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar. Record the count from each.
3. **Combine and de-duplicate.** The same paper appears in several databases. Reference managers can remove duplicates.
4. **Screen** by title and abstract: relevant or not. Record how many you drop.
5. **Full read** of the survivors: check the method really does what the abstract claims. Keep the final set.

Draw it as a funnel flowchart (the PRISMA style used in systematic reviews).

Made-up example: Scopus 200 + ScienceDirect 50 + Web of Science 50 = 300 → minus 50 duplicates = 250 → title/abstract screening keeps 40 → full read keeps 12.

The lecturer said this skill carries over to a master's thesis and to publishing review papers.
