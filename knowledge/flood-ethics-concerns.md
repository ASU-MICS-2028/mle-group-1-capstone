# Flood ethics concerns

The central issue is **unequal visibility** in news-based labels.

- **Rural under-reporting.** Sparse labels in Oti or Ahafo may reflect few reporters, not few floods. A model trained on them may under-warn the communities least able to cope.
- **Accra dominance.** With 38% of events, Greater Accra can dominate overall metrics. Report every region separately.
- **Proxy effects.** Built-up share and population may stand in for media attention and wealth. Test predictions with them removed.
- **Language.** Local-language and radio news may be missing; coverage in Ghana is unconfirmed — investigate for the datasheet.
- **Alert fatigue and false comfort.** Too many alerts get ignored; a low score must not be read as "safe". The label is reported floods, not risk.
- **Eviction risk.** Maps marking informal settlements as "flood risk" could be used to justify removals, given the documented link between flooding and informal urbanisation in Accra (Amoako & Inkoom 2017). Present any map with care, especially in the F3 fallback.

**Proposed mitigation:** audit coverage against satellite records; per-region alert thresholds so rural regions are not always below the cut-off; show uncertainty on the dashboard; write down what the tool must not be used for.
