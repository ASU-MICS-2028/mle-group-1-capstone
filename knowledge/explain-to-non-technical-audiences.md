# Explain results to non-technical audiences

The lecturer listed communication as a learning outcome. His point: many scientists and engineers cannot explain their work to people who are not technical, so the course trains and grades it. It is shown through the prosit presentations, video presentations and the final capstone defence.

In practice the prosits were presented to mock panels (e.g. a mock Ministry of Health panel). The lecturer encouraged plots and visuals over walls of numbers.

Tips that follow from this:
- Start with the decision the audience must make, not the algorithm.
- Turn metrics into plain consequences ("of 10 real floods, we warn for 7").
- One clear chart beats a table of ten scores.

## For the capstone
Our audience is NADMO regional staff. The dashboard and talk should say what an alert means, how often it is wrong, and what it cannot see (unreported floods).
