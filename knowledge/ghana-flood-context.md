# Ghana flood context

- **3 June 2015, Accra:** floods and a fuel-station fire killed about **159** people, per the Vice-President's count at the time (Graphic Online, https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/death-toll-in-june-3-disaster-now-159-veep.html). Published totals vary depending on whether fire victims are counted.
- **2023 Akosombo Dam controlled spillage**, 15 Sep–30 Oct (Newscenta, https://newscenta.com/vras-spillage-management-chronology-revealed): **more than 26,000** people displaced according to NADMO figures reported in the press (Ghanaian Times). Figures vary by date and definition — **check NADMO or IFRC reports before quoting.**
- **Causes are structural, not only rain:** informal urbanisation, drainage and land use — Amoako C & Inkoom DKB (2017), *Urban Studies* 55, 2903–2922, doi:10.1177/0042098016686526; Amoako C & Frimpong Boamah E (2014), *Int. J. Urban Sustainable Development* 7, 109–129, doi:10.1080/19463138.2014.984720.
- **Wider picture:** the share of the world's population exposed to floods is growing (Tellman et al. 2021, doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03695-w); WMO has said about **60% of Africa lacks early-warning coverage** (reported by Down To Earth).
- **Seasonality:** two rainy seasons, with flood-report peaks in June and September–October.

All casualty and displacement figures come from press reports and are **unverified against primary sources**.
