# Source or stress-test every assumed number

Two cases, two prosits:

- **Prosit 1:** our allocation used a budget of 1,000,000 bed nets. It was our own assumption, not a sourced figure, and we lost a point on framing (our reading; no comments).
- **Prosit 2:** the cost matrix assumed a repaying customer was worth 400 KES of future business. Later we *measured* it from first-time borrowers who repaid: a mean of 174–228 KES, so about 200 KES, half the guess. A fairness fix that looked like it saved 7.7 KES per loan saved only about 3.4 at the measured value.

Every headline number in a decision should be one of:
1. **sourced** (cite the document or dataset);
2. **measured** from our data (with the table saved);
3. **assumed**, labelled as such, with a sensitivity range showing whether the decision changes.

Where possible, measure the guess. It was cheap in Prosit 2 and changed the story.

## For the capstone
The cost of a missed flood warning versus a false alarm, and any "budget" of alerts per week, must be cited (NADMO, World Bank, published studies) or explicitly assumed with a sensitivity table. Don't invent a figure and leave it unlabelled.
